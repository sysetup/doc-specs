# Risk management policy specification

## Identity and selection

- **Specification ID:** `POLICY@risk-management`.
- **Purpose:** Establish mandatory direction for assessing, owning, treating, accepting, and reviewing risks to stated objectives.
- **Intended readers:** People who identify or own risks, decide treatment or acceptance, oversee affected objectives, and issue or maintain the policy.
- **Decision or action supported:** Readers can determine which risk criteria and authorities apply, when a risk requires escalation or treatment, and what constitutes an authorized acceptance decision.
- **Use when:** An issuing authority needs common risk management obligations across a defined organization, project, or service boundary.

## Authoring inputs and unresolved facts

Obtain the issuing mandate and supplied project objectives, risk categories, time horizons, organizational and lifecycle boundaries. For the subjects in scope, obtain the actual risk criteria and assessment method, their established project basis or internal authority to establish them, tolerances and escalation thresholds; roles for risk identification, ownership, treatment decisions, and acceptance; the record and communication expectations for risk decisions; and monitoring and review triggers. Obtain exception and policy change authority, and the edition, issuance state, and effective-date decision when established.

If risk criteria, thresholds, authority, or scope are unknown or undecided, identify the gap, its effect on prioritization or acceptance, the resolving action, and an actual owner if assigned. Do not invent numerical scales or treat a low rating as permission to accept. A draft without established authority, scope, or substantive obligations MUST remain visibly proposed. Do not invent risk entries, treatment outcomes, acceptance decisions, or review events.

Normally all five named risk subjects are covered. A subject may be excluded only where it is outside the issuing mandate for the stated scope. For each exclusion, record the subject, exact excluded scope, reason, actual issuing authority, explicit disposition, and decision date or locator. A pending disposition does not remove a subject or reduce its obligations; the policy MUST remain proposed until that disposition is established. Only an approved exclusion of an entire named subject reduces the coverage and directive minimum; a partial exclusion leaves that subject in scope. At least one named subject and substantive mandatory direction MUST remain. If all subjects are excluded, keep the document proposed and resolve its mandate and scope before issuance.

## Finished-document contract

- **Title:** Identify the governed organization, project, or service where needed and name the document as a risk management policy.
- **Frontmatter:** None. Begin with the GFM title. Authority, scope, and policy state belong in the body.
- **Client or receiving party:** Conditional body element in the identity or scope section. Include it only when a named external party commissions or receives the work; then state that party's established name and relationship, such as client or receiving organization. When no external party exists, state that the document has no external client. Do not invent a party. Do not put the party in the specification ID, filename, or type name.
- **Order:** State authority, scope, and objectives before risk obligations; follow with decision rights and exceptions, then communication and policy review. Headings may use local wording if the roles below remain identifiable.

| Section role | Status and inclusion condition | Content obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Authority, purpose, and applicability | Required | Identify the real issuer or mark it proposed; policy owner, edition, truthful issuance state, covered objectives, people and activities, organizational and time boundaries, exclusions, and effective date only when authorized. Explain the risk governance outcome and the project objectives or decisions that establish the obligations. |
| Mandatory risk direction | Required; subject coverage follows the approved exclusions defined above | State separately identifiable obligations for **risk criteria**, **risk ownership**, **treatment**, **acceptance**, and **risk review** wherever each subject remains in scope. For those subjects, respectively govern when risks must be identified and assessed and how a consistent method and escalation thresholds are established for the covered objectives; how each risk receives an accountable owner; who selects and authorizes treatment; what residual exposure must be considered before acceptance; and what conditions require monitoring, reassessment, or escalation. State how cross-boundary risks or criteria conflicts within scope are resolved. |
| Risk decisions and accountability | Required | For the subjects in scope, allocate authority for criteria, ownership, treatment, acceptance, and escalation, including decisions above a role's mandate. Distinguish the risk owner from an action performer and from the acceptance authority when their duties differ. Where acceptance is covered, define its minimum recorded basis, including the accepted scope, conditions, and review point. A proposed treatment, assessment, or silence MUST NOT be described as acceptance. |
| Exceptions and noncompliance | Required | State whether exceptions to the policy are allowed. If allowed, define decision authority, request basis and risk consequence, scope, duration or review condition, how the decision is recorded, and interim treatment. If prohibited, state the conflict route. Explain escalation of failure to apply the policy. Accepting a risk MUST NOT silently waive an established project requirement or grant a policy exception. |
| Communication, oversight, and policy review | Required | State how in-scope risk obligations, including criteria where covered, and policy changes reach affected roles, what risk and treatment information within scope reaches decision authorities, and the cadence or triggers and authority for changing the policy. Keep review of individual risks distinct from review of this policy. |

## Content definitions and forms

| Element | Meaning and cardinality | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Issuing authority and policy owner | One identified issuer or delegated role and one maintenance role. | An operational risk owner is not automatically the issuing authority; label a proposed issuer as proposed. |
| Applicability and objectives | One explicit scope statement covering objectives, organizational or system boundary, affected roles, lifecycle or time horizon, and material exclusions. | State whether the policy covers adverse risks only or also opportunity-related uncertainty; do not silently extend decision rights across boundaries. |
| Issuance state and edition | One truthful state and unambiguous edition; effective date only when decided. | Distinguish proposed, authorized but not yet effective, effective, and withdrawn or superseded in local terms. |
| Risk directive | Normally five or more distinct obligations, with at least one for each named subject. Subtract one from this minimum for each approved whole-subject exclusion; retain at least one distinct obligation per remaining subject. | Give each a stable local label, addressee, required action or constraint, and applicable condition. Count a directive only once even if it covers several subjects; such coverage does not reduce the distinct-obligation minimum. Subject labels, exclusions, unresolved gaps, and aspirations do not count. |
| Risk criteria and escalation rule | When risk criteria remain in scope, one coherent rule for establishing or adopting an assessment method and decision thresholds for the covered objectives. | Identify who may approve the method, its objective and time horizon, how likelihood and consequence or another method are interpreted, and when exposure requires escalation. State the method's assessment rules and thresholds explicitly. Quantified scales are conditional on the actual method. A score alone never grants acceptance authority. |
| Risk ownership and treatment rule | When risk ownership remains in scope, at least one rule for assigning an accountable owner; when treatment remains in scope, at least one rule for selecting, authorizing, and monitoring treatment. | For ownership, define how an unassigned or cross-boundary risk is escalated. For treatment, state how it is proposed, authorized, monitored to completion, and reassessed; identify how forecast residual exposure and observed reassessment inform decisions. Apply both constraints when both subjects are covered. |
| Acceptance rule | When acceptance remains in scope, at least one rule identifying who may accept which residual exposure and on what recorded basis. | Define any tolerance, conditions, expiry or reassessment trigger, and escalation above authority. An owner or assessor may accept only if the real mandate grants that power. Require an explicit decision within that mandate. |
| Review rule | When risk review remains in scope, at least one obligation for risk monitoring and reassessment. One policy review rule is always required. | For risk review, name triggers such as material change, treatment result, threshold breach, or approaching decision expiry as applicable; set a usable cadence or authority for setting one. Keep risk review and policy review obligations separately identifiable. |
| Exception rule | One policy-exception allowance or prohibition. | For permitted policy exceptions, define authority and conditions, decision recording, and interim treatment; for prohibited exceptions, define the conflict route. When risk acceptance remains in scope, define its authority and conditions separately. A change to an established project requirement requires a separate decision by its actual change authority; identify that authority and conditions where the decision is within the issuing mandate and stated scope. A policy exception MUST NOT authorize risk acceptance or a requirement change outside that scope or mandate. |

Use concise prose and numbered directives. A compact decision-rights table MAY clarify several authorities, and a brief escalation flow MAY clarify threshold or cross-boundary cases.

## Quality criteria

- All five risk subjects are covered unless an explicit issuer-approved exclusion removes a subject for the stated scope. Every exclusion has its scoped reason and decision identity; pending decisions and empty governed scope prevent issuance. The distinct-directive minimum matches the remaining subjects without counting exclusions or gaps.
- Subjects in scope form a coherent governance rule: where covered, criteria support assessment and escalation, every active risk can be assigned or escalated, treatment and acceptance rights are explicit, and risk review can respond to change. Policy review remains required for every scope.
- A reader can identify which method and decision authority apply to the subjects within the stated objectives and scope. Undefined scales, conflicting thresholds, or missing ownership needed for those subjects remain visible gaps rather than plausible values.
- For treatment, reassessment, or acceptance within scope, the rules define the required decision basis and authority. Policy-exception rules always define that basis and authority. No risk is treated as accepted merely because it was scored, reviewed, or left without action.
- Each directive states mandatory risk management direction for the covered objectives and boundaries.
