# Safety assurance plan specification

## Identity and selection

- **Specification ID:** `SAFETY-ASSURANCE-PLAN@conditional`.
- **Purpose:** Plan how a project will identify and control safety hazards, establish safety obligations, obtain assurance evidence, review residual exposure, and sustain safety controls across its applicable lifecycle.
- **Intended readers:** Project and safety leads, engineering and operations teams, affected suppliers, assurance reviewers, and the actual authorities for safety decisions.
- **Decision or action supported:** Assign and coordinate safety analyses, controls, verification, reviews, and decision gates before work or operation continues under an applicable safety obligation.
- **Use when:** Established project needs, allocated requirements, decisions, or supplied agreement scope identify a safety obligation for the project's system, service, or activity, and coordinated safety work needs a plan.
- **Scope boundaries:** Cover safety hazard analysis, control work, planned assurance evidence, decision gates, and lifecycle reassessment for the selected system, service, or activity.

## Authoring inputs and unresolved facts

Obtain established project safety needs, allocated requirements, decisions, and supplied agreement scope; system and lifecycle boundary; intended use, affected people or environment, operating conditions, and interfaces; available hazard information and the method for finding further hazards; explicit risk criteria and safety decision roles; architecture and controlled configurations; safety requirements and controls already established; planned analyses and verification methods; supplier, operations, maintenance, and retirement responsibilities; change and incident routes; and assigned receiving-party review or acceptance interfaces. Identify the project records supporting each obligation by ID, version, and accessible locator.

For unknown facts, state what is missing, its effect on the plan, the resolving action, and the actual owner if assigned. Mark an unmade method, criterion, or authority as not established. Explain genuine inapplicability against the stated scope and established project safety obligation or a real authorized tailoring decision; do not infer exemption from silence. An absent safety basis, risk criterion, accountable decision authority, or credible route to control and verify material hazards prevents a claim that the affected work is ready for a conclusive safety decision. Identify planned evidence and pending decisions by their actual state; cite existing evidence or decisions only with their record locators. Do not invent hazard closure, independent review, approval, or accepted risk.

## Finished-document contract

- **Title:** Identify the project or system and name its safety assurance plan.
- **Frontmatter:** None. Begin with the GFM title. Applicability, configuration, and decision authority are substantive body content.
- **Client or receiving party:** Conditional body element in the identity or scope section. Include it only when a named external party commissions or receives the work; then state that party's established name and relationship, such as client or receiving organization. When no external party exists, state that the document has no external client. Do not invent a party. Do not put the party in the specification ID, filename, or type name.
- **Order:** Establish the obligation and subject before analysis and controls; place planned evidence before safety decision gates; close with lifecycle upkeep. Heading wording may vary.

| Section role | Status and inclusion condition | Content obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Scope, obligation, and responsibility | Required | Identify the safety-relevant system or activity, intended use and lifecycle boundary, each established project safety obligation and its project-record ID and version, exclusions and their basis, plan owner, participating roles, and the actual authorities for technical disposition and residual-risk decisions. Include a safety classification only when the project has established it; state its meaning, scope, and assignment basis. |
| Hazard and risk approach | Required | Define how hazards, causes, affected parties, operating conditions, interfaces, and changes will be identified and analyzed; the selected risk method and explicit criteria; who performs, reviews, and updates analyses; and how unresolved or newly discovered hazards are escalated. Select analysis techniques, including FMEA when appropriate, according to the project's hazard characteristics and analysis needs, and explain the selection. |
| Safety requirements and control work | Required | Explain how hazard controls become allocated, assessable safety requirements or operational restrictions; how design, implementation, supplier, and operations work will maintain them; and how each material change is checked for effects on hazards and controls. Distinguish proposed controls from authorized baselines. |
| Assurance evidence and review | Required | Map established safety requirements and controls, or a defined rule for future ones, to planned inspection, analysis, test, demonstration, or other justified evidence. Specify target configuration, criterion, responsible role, review method, gaps, and independence where assigned by a project requirement or justified by risk. When a project obligation or decision requires a safety argument or case, explain how claims, evidence, assumptions, and gaps will be assembled and reviewed. State how conflicting evidence, or evidence that does not meet its criterion or cannot be concluded, will be handled. Identify expected evidence and planned reviews with their planning state, dependencies, and completion criteria. |
| Safety gates and residual-exposure disposition | Required | Define entry and exit criteria for the relevant design, test, delivery, or operational safety decisions; the evidence package and review roles; how open hazards, limitations, restrictions, and residual exposure reach the actual authority; and the route when criteria are unmet. Identify each gate's planning state and unresolved prerequisites, the restrictions pending a decision, and the record needed to establish its later decision. |
| Lifecycle coordination and upkeep | Required | Plan supplier and handoff interfaces, operator information and restrictions, maintenance and configuration control, incident or anomaly learning, reassessment triggers, and retirement or disposal concerns where safety remains relevant. If a security event can create or change a hazard, name the safety-security interface, the reassessment trigger, the hazard conditions addressed by the security control, and the additional safety evidence needed. If no such event can affect a hazard, say so. State how the plan and assurance basis are updated after changes. |
| Receiving-party safety review or acceptance interface | Conditional when supplied project scope or agreement assigns a safety submission, review, or acceptance interface to a receiving party | Identify the assigned obligation, submission or review product, timing, decision role, response to conditions or findings, and evidence needed for a later decision. |
| Terms and supporting material | Conditional when local risk terms, analysis rules, or controlled project records are needed to interpret a criterion | Define the necessary terms and analysis rules, including how they affect the criterion; identify supporting project records by accessible locator and version, and protect sensitive details where appropriate. |

## Content definitions and forms

| Element | Meaning and cardinality | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Safety basis | At least one established project safety obligation for the selected plan. | State the obligation, affected scope, supporting project need, allocation, decision, or supplied agreement scope, its record ID and version, and any actual project tailoring authority. |
| Safety-relevant subject | One bounded system, service, or activity, with multiple configurations or operating modes when material. | State who or what may be harmed and where interfaces or lifecycle transitions alter the exposure. |
| Hazard analysis route | One or more planned analyses, with an update route for newly identified hazards. | Give purpose, method or selection rule, inputs, timing, responsible role, review, and treatment of uncertainty; do not seed fictional hazard rows. |
| Risk criterion and disposition route | One explicit project decision basis and actual authority for each kind of residual-risk decision. | State established severity and likelihood definitions, tolerance thresholds, and acceptance conditions with their project-record basis; identify missing criteria as unresolved. Identify the decision and evidence required to accept residual exposure, including who may decide it. |
| Control-to-evidence link | One planned assessment route for each established material safety requirement or control, plus a rule for future additions. | Bind hazard or requirement, control, target configuration, criterion, method, responsible role, and expected evidence; expose uncovered or provisional links. |
| Safety argument or case | Conditional when an established project obligation or decision requires a structured safety claim. | Plan its claim scope, assumptions, supporting analyses and evidence, review, limitations, and update triggers; distinguish planned support from evidence already available. |
| Safety gate | One or more gates where safety evidence affects continuation, restriction, delivery, or operation. | State inputs, explicit criterion, decision maker, restrictions pending a decision, the route when a criterion is unmet, and the record a later decision would produce. Identify separately the plan's approval state and each safety gate's planned or pending state. |
| Lifecycle trigger | One or more change, anomaly, incident, operational, or retirement conditions that require reassessment. | Name the route for updating analyses, controls, evidence, restrictions, and stakeholders. |

Use prose for rationale and authority, and a flow or ordered list for analysis-to-control-to-decision work. Use a trace table with meaningful columns when several hazards, requirements, or controls are already known; otherwise define the mapping rule without inventing entries. A gate table may clarify decision inputs and owners. Identify an existing controlled hazard record or analysis by version and accessible locator when available.

## Quality criteria

- The selected safety obligation, lifecycle scope, analysis rigor, control work, and decision rights agree; a generic safety aspiration cannot substitute for an applicable basis.
- Known material hazards and safety requirements have a planned control and assessment route, while unknowns, provisional controls, and evidence gaps remain visible.
- Planned reviews, independence, receiving-party submissions, and restrictions have explicit project needs or decisions, inclusion conditions, and assigned decision roles.
- Proposed work, observed safety evidence, hazard disposition, plan approval, and residual-risk acceptance have explicit states; any existing evidence or decision has a locator and affected configuration, and pending work has a completion or decision route.
- Where a security event can create or change a hazard, the plan names the safety-security interface, the reassessment trigger, the hazard conditions addressed by the security control, and the additional safety evidence needed. Where none can, the plan says so.
