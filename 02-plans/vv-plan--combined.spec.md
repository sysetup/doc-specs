# Verification and validation plan specification

## Identity and selection

- **Specification ID:** `VV-PLAN@combined`.
- **Purpose:** Coordinate verification of specified technical obligations and validation of stakeholder or intended-use needs in one bounded project plan, while preserving separate assessment bases, criteria, and conclusions.
- **Intended readers:** V&V leads and performers, technical and stakeholder representatives, assurance reviewers, and people deciding readiness, disposition, or receipt of results.
- **Decision or action supported:** Agree what will be assessed on each path, with which configuration, methods, resources, criteria, and evidence, and how the two paths will be coordinated and reported.
- **Use when:** One project plan covers both verification and validation for the same system or program scope.
- **Scope boundaries:** Coordinate planned assessment activities, resources, criteria, and evidence routes for both paths. A shared event may serve both paths only when each path has its own basis and criterion.

## Authoring inputs and unresolved facts

Obtain the real system or item boundary and planned configuration, in-scope project technical requirements with editions and status, stakeholder needs or intended-use statements and their origins, project decisions and decision rights, whether independence is required by an established project decision or justified by risk, assessment levels, risk priorities, candidate methods and criteria, operational scenarios, facilities and environments, participants, tools, schedule dependencies, evidence controls, and actual planned certification or receiving-party products and handoffs. Read the supplied requirement and need statements and identify the project need, allocation, or decision establishing their scope. Existing project matrices, procedures, and lifecycle records may supply mapping, method, or sequencing data.

Mark an unavailable fact as unknown and an unmade choice as not established. For each material gap, state the affected path or decision, consequence, resolving action, and actual owner if assigned. Explain a genuinely inapplicable level or deliverable; do not invent a program tier, certification duty, requirement, need, threshold, approval, evidence, or result. State independence for each path as required, with the established project decision or justified risk and the role separation; as not required, because neither applies; or as not established, when that basis is unknown. Do not treat an unknown project decision as a decision that independence is unnecessary, and do not invent an independent role. Missing assessment bases, criteria, target-configuration rules, or essential decision authority prevent a claim that the affected path is ready for later conclusive work.

## Finished-document contract

- **Title:** Identify the system or project scope and name the document as its verification and validation plan.
- **Frontmatter:** None. Begin with the GFM title. Scope, editions, and decision rules are substantive body content.
- **Client or receiving party:** Conditional body element in the identity or scope section. Include it only when a named external party commissions or receives the work; then state that party's established name and relationship, such as client or receiving organization. When no external party exists, state that the document has no external client. Do not invent a party. Do not put the party in the specification ID, filename, or type name.
- **Order:** Establish subject and both assessment bases before methods; place resources and sequence before evidence and result interfaces. Heading wording may vary. Keep the two mappings visibly distinct even when logistics are shared.

| Section role | Status and inclusion condition | Content obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose, scope, and project basis | Required | Identify the system or item, lifecycle and project boundary, objectives of both paths, included and excluded work, requirement and need origins, editions and locators, and established project commitments versus proposed statements or contextual information where that distinction affects decisions. Explain material exclusions and unresolved conflicts among project requirements, needs, or decisions. |
| Assessment subjects and levels | Required | Define how target configurations, interfaces, enabling products, and end-item or integrated-system subjects will be selected and identified. State which levels apply and why; include cross-project or program-level coordination only when it exists. Do not require a separate section for every possible level. |
| Verification strategy and requirement mapping | Required | For each in-scope technical obligation, give a suitable inspection, analysis, test, demonstration, or other justified method; assessment level, target configuration, criterion, responsible role, planned event or method reference, and expected evidence when those facts are established. Leave a missing element as an explicit gap or deferral. Do not invent it, and do not enter a result. |
| Validation strategy and need mapping | Required | For each in-scope stakeholder or intended-use need, give a representative scenario or use condition, actor or justified proxy, evaluation method, observable criterion, configuration, responsible role, and expected evidence when those facts are established. State fidelity and coverage limits, and leave a missing element as an explicit gap. A technical conformance row cannot answer a fitness question, and the mapping has no result. |
| Integrated execution and readiness | Required | Coordinate sequence, dependencies, milestones or windows, facilities, data, tools and calibration when material, participants, competence, and entry, suspension, resumption, and exit rules. State the independence conclusion for each path: required, with the established project decision or justified risk and the role separation; not required, because neither applies; or not established, when that basis is unknown. State who decides readiness and how a change to either basis or configuration affects both paths. |
| Evidence, anomalies, and reporting | Required | Define capture and review rules binding actual observations to subject and configuration, requirement or need and method editions, criteria, participants or tools, deviations, and review. Define event and attempt states for planned reporting: `not-run` applies only to an activity or scenario selected for a bounded event that received no assessment action; unselected planned work has no attempt state. An attempted assessment can receive `pass`, `fail`, or `inconclusive`. A missing observation or an invalidating condition on an attempt requires `inconclusive`; do not classify it as `not-run` or introduce a separate invalid result. Preserve each retest as a distinct later attempt linked to the earlier result. Define separate reporting of verification coverage and conclusions and validation coverage and conclusions, even when one event serves both paths, and identify how technical findings, document approval, and receiving-party decisions will be recorded and communicated. |
| Certification or acceptance interfaces | Conditional: an actual planned project certification or receiving-party decision requires products | Name the established project delivery obligation, decision role and recipient, planned evidence package and handoff, and any review gate. State expected products, handoff criteria, and the decision to be supported. |
| Terms and acronyms | Conditional: local terms could make a criterion or assessment path ambiguous | Define only terms needed to interpret this plan, especially any project-specific use of verification, validation, level, or result. An appendix is optional. |

## Content definitions and forms

| Element | Meaning and cardinality | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Planned subject and configuration | One bounded system or set of items and a rule for selecting each assessed baseline. | A target may still be planned, but the rule must bind actual events to a known configuration later. |
| Verification basis | One or more in-scope project technical obligations or requirement clauses when their wording is established; otherwise one explicit source gap. | Identify the supplied project requirement record, edition, and locator as needed; show proposal or unresolved status rather than silently treating it as approved. An identifier whose wording was not inspected is a gap, not an obligation. |
| Validation basis | One or more project stakeholder needs, mission outcomes, or intended-use questions when they are established; otherwise one explicit source gap. | Identify the real project need origin and use context; do not replace a need with a technical requirement ID alone or invent the missing need. |
| Verification mapping | One visible account for every in-scope technical obligation or separately assessable facet. | Show method, level, criterion, configuration, responsibility, planned event, and expected evidence when established. An in-scope obligation that lacks one of those stays an explicit gap or deferral; do not invent the missing element. A planned row has no result. |
| Validation mapping | One visible account for every in-scope need or distinct use question. | Show representative scenario and conditions, actor or proxy, evaluation criterion, method, configuration, responsibility, and expected evidence when established. An in-scope question that lacks one of those stays an explicit gap; do not invent the actor, conditions, or criterion. A planned row has no result. |
| Assessment criterion | One observable rule for each obligation or use question that has an established criterion; none when the criterion is still a gap. | Give established limits, units, conditions, and judgment rule where material. Do not invent thresholds or call a favorable observation formal acceptance. |
| Shared event | Zero or more events that may later collect evidence for both paths. | Keep a separate basis, criterion, and later result link for each path. Sharing an event assigns neither path's result and does not merge conclusions. |
| Planned evidence package | One defined route for each planned assessment and any real recipient obligation. | State artifact type, configuration and method binding, custody or locator, review, and retention rule when established. Expected artifacts are not actual evidence. |

Provide two distinct mapping views. Use separate tables with meaningful columns when there are several obligations or needs; for a short scope, explicit mapped lists are acceptable. An existing controlled project matrix may be referenced by edition and locator with a coverage summary, without copying uncontrolled rows. Use a schedule or flow only when it clarifies dependencies between levels and paths. Prose should explain strategy, rationale, limitations, and decision authority.

## Quality criteria

- Both paths have distinct assessment bases and reporting routes. A shared resource or event does not erase that distinction or merge a later conformance conclusion with a later fitness conclusion.
- A future conclusion is bounded by the chosen configuration, method, conditions, coverage, and evidence limits. This plan states no such conclusion, and gaps remain visible.
- The independence conclusion for each path is required, not required, or not established. Silence is not a decision that independence is unnecessary.
- Sequencing, resources, and readiness rules are feasible or explicitly unresolved, and a change can be traced into each affected path.
- Program-level and certification content follows actual project applicability.
- This plan assigns no `pass`, `fail`, `inconclusive`, or `not-run`. Identify planned technical disposition, document approval, and receiving-party decision roles and gates without claiming completed assessments or decisions.
