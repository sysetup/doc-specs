# Cybersecurity incident record specification

## Identity and selection

- **Specification ID:** `INCIDENT-RECORD@cybersecurity`.
- **Purpose:** Record one actual or suspected cybersecurity incident: its detection, affected resources, indicators, containment, eradication, and notification duties, together with its chronology, evidence handling, recovery status, and closure.
- **Intended readers:** The incident lead, security responders, affected resource owners, and the roles that decide containment, eradication, notification, and closure.
- **Decision or action supported:** Direct containment and eradication, account for notification duties, and decide whether the incident remains open using its actual response history and evidence.
- **Use when:** A suspected or confirmed cybersecurity incident needs a contemporaneous record of detection, scope, response actions, notification, and evidence.
- **Scope boundaries:** Cover an actual or suspected cybersecurity incident and the response performed for it. Ordinary vulnerability handling before suspected exploitation is outside the scope. Causal analysis, failed-control conclusions, lessons, and dwell-time analysis are excluded from this contemporaneous record.

## Authoring inputs and unresolved facts

Obtain the current situation, detection source, vector, and confidence basis; the assigned declaration role and project severity categories; incident command and permitted actions; each affected resource and the evidence of its effect; any observed indicators and their handling limits; each actual containment action and its authorization; eradication state and evidence; established project notification assignments, with recipients, deadlines or triggers, disclosure decision roles, and sending evidence that actually exist; the chronology; evidence custody; service-recovery state; and the project closure criteria and any actual closure decision.

For each notification duty, use the supplied project assignment or decision that establishes its recipients and deadline or trigger, and give its locator. If that assignment or decision is unavailable, record the determination as unresolved. Do not invent recipients, deadlines, or duties. If a fact is unknown or not decided, state that, its consequence, the resolving action, and the actual owner if assigned. Do not invent an indicator, a vector, a confidence value, a resource, a containment action, an eradication result, or a sent notice.

## Finished-document contract

- **Title:** Name the incident and identify the document as its cybersecurity incident record.
- **Frontmatter:** None. Begin with the GFM title. Detection, scope, and status belong in the body.
- **Client or receiving party:** Conditional body element in the identity or scope section. Include it only when a named external party commissions or receives the work; then state that party's established name and relationship, such as client or receiving organization. When no external party exists, state that the document has no external client. Do not invent a party. Do not put the party in the specification ID, filename, or type name.
- **Order:** State declaration, then detection and affected scope, then the chronology. Place containment and eradication before notification. Place evidence handling before recovery and closure. Heading wording may vary. These roles are record content.

| Section role | Status and inclusion condition | Content obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Declaration and command | Required | Give the incident a stable identity and the as-of time of this version. State whether it is suspected, declared, or determined not to be a cybersecurity incident, with the authority and the project's severity or category. Identify the lead, authorized responders, target environment, and permitted actions. |
| Detection and affected scope | Required | State the detection source, describe how the incident presented or entered with its vector assessment as confirmed, hypothesized, or unresolved, and give confidence with its basis and scale. Confidence is about that characterization, not a substitute for severity. For each identified resource, state the effect as observed or suspected and the evidence, or that the evidence is unresolved. If no resource is identified, distinguish unresolved scope from an assessment finding no affected resources, with its scope and evidence. State whether indicators were not assessed, none are established, or list each observed indicator. |
| Chronology | Required | Append observations, decisions, actions, communications, and corrections in recording order, preserving each event's occurrence time separately. A late-reported event states that delay; unknown occurrence times remain unknown. A correction identifies the earlier event and the change. Label hypotheses. For each event, state the actor and authority basis, or that the actor or authority basis is unknown, and state the impact known then, that no additional impact was established, or that impact is unknown. |
| Containment and eradication | Required | Record each containment action that was performed, with its scope, authority, time or explicit time uncertainty, and observed effect or unresolved evidence. Label a planned or authorized-but-not-performed action as such. If none was performed, say so and state the current exposure consequence. State current containment separately from the action history. State eradication as not assessed, not started, in progress, completed, or not applicable, with the basis. Describe eradication work actually performed, including partial work, and distinguish planned work. Completed eradication identifies what was removed or remediated, the supporting checks and evidence, and what uncertainty remains. |
| Notification | Required | State whether project notification duties are identified, none apply, or the determination is unresolved, and give the project assignment, decision, or assessed scope supporting that position. For each identified duty, state the audience, the deadline or trigger, the project assignment or decision establishing them and its locator, the status, and the assigned disclosure decision role. `sent` requires sending evidence. `pending` and `not-sent` are not evidence of sending. |
| Evidence handling | Required | State what was collected or that nothing was collected. Cover collection authority, provenance, integrity, custody and transfers, confidentiality, retention, and access. Do not place secrets, credentials, or unnecessary sensitive indicator values in the record; give a locator and the handling restriction. |
| Recovery and closure | Required | State service recovery separately from eradication and from closure. If the incident is open, say that closure has not occurred and identify remaining issues. If it is closed, state the project closure criteria, the actual deciding role and decision, and the time. Give the locator of any existing review relevant to closure. |

## Content definitions and forms

| Element | Meaning and cardinality | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Incident identity | One stable identifier for this incident. | Do not reuse it for another incident. The as-of time is a date-time with an explicit offset, or the clock time is stated as unknown, with clock confidence when a time is given. |
| Declaration | One current position: `suspected`, `declared`, or `determined-not-a-cyber-incident`. | Name the assigned declaration role. A suspected incident stays suspected until that role declares or rejects it. Reclassification states the actual decision and deciding role, with a locator to any resulting incident record that exists. Define the project severity categories used, or record the unresolved scale. |
| Detection | One current characterization. | The source is the actual alert, report, sensor, or person, or an explicit unresolved source. Describe how the incident presented or entered, or state that this is unknown; its assessment is `confirmed`, `hypothesized`, or `unresolved`. The assessment label is not the vector itself. Confidence names its scale or says that no scale is established, and it includes the evidence basis. Do not convert uncertainty into a confirmed vector. |
| Affected resource | Zero or more identified resources, plus one scope statement. | Each resource has an identity, an effect marked `observed` or `suspected`, and evidence or an unresolved-evidence statement. Zero resources requires either explicit unresolved scope or a finding that none were affected within a stated assessed scope, with evidence and limitations. A reasonably believed effect is `suspected`. |
| Indicator | Zero or more observed indicators, plus one assessment. | The assessment is `not-assessed`, `none-established`, or `listed`. Each listed indicator has a type, the value as observed, where and when it was seen, and why it is relevant. Restrict sensitive values. An empty list without the assessment is not "none". |
| Containment action | Zero or more performed actions. | Each performed action has the action, scope, authority or an explicit unknown authority, a time or explicit unknown, and the observed effect or unresolved evidence. Known timestamps use date-times with offset and clock confidence; retain partial times or bounds without inventing precision. Planned actions are labeled `planned` and are not performed actions. |
| Containment state | One current state. | State contained, not contained, unknown, or not applicable, with assessed scope and evidence or its gap. Inapplicability requires evidence that no harmful condition needs containment in that scope. Performing a containment action does not itself establish containment. |
| Eradication state and work | Exactly one of `not-assessed`, `not-started`, `in-progress`, `completed`, or `not-applicable`, with an account of actual work. | Identify performed work, its scope and results or evidence gaps, even when incomplete; distinguish plans. `completed` cites what was removed or remediated, the supporting checks and evidence, and the remaining uncertainty. `not-applicable` states the reason. Eradication is not containment, service recovery, or closure. |
| Notification duty | Zero or more identified project duties, plus one applicability statement. | Applicability is `duties-identified`, `none-apply`, or `unresolved`, with the supporting project assignment, decision, or assessed scope. Each duty has an audience, a deadline or trigger, the project assignment or decision establishing them and its locator, a status of `not-assessed`, `pending`, `sent`, or `not-sent`, and the assigned disclosure decision role. `sent` cites evidence. Do not invent an audience to fill a row. |
| Timeline event | One or more events. | Each has a stable local identity, a description, an occurrence time and a recording time, each with an explicit unknown when unavailable. Known timestamps use date-times with offset and clock confidence; retain partial times or bounds without inventing precision. Types are `observation`, `decision`, `action`, `communication`, and `correction`. Each event states the actor and the authority basis, or states that the actor or authority basis is unknown. The actor is a person, role, or tool. Do not invent a person, role, or tool, or an authority. The opening of the record may be the first event when the occurrence time is unknown, and it must say so. A notification marked `sent` and a communication event for that notice must agree on audience and time when both exist. |
| Evidence account | One handling account. | Separate material not collected, material collected, and material whose custody is unknown. Record actual custody and transfer facts or their gaps; an intended handling step does not establish that custody occurred. |
| Recovery state | One current service state. | State restored, partially restored, not restored, unknown, or not applicable, and the basis. Inapplicability requires evidence that no service needs restoration in the assessed scope. State the scope and outcome of actual restoration checks, including unresolved service limitations. |
| Closure | One current position, `open` or `closed`. | `open` does not name a closing authority. `closed` cites criteria, authority, and time. Lessons, dwell time, and failed-control conclusions are outside this record. |

Use prose for detection, eradication, and evidence handling. Use tables for resources, indicators, containment actions, notification duties, and chronology when several items must be compared; every row is a real item or is omitted under the assessment rule above. Do not add an empty row or a generic document-control block.

## Quality criteria

- Detection source, vector, and confidence are present or visibly unresolved. Confidence does not stand in for severity, and a hypothesis is not recorded as a confirmed vector.
- Affected resources, indicators, containment, and notification duties are explicitly assessed. Absence is stated. It is not implied by a missing section and not filled with a fabricated row.
- A performed containment action has authority or an explicit unknown, a time or explicit uncertainty, and an effect or unresolved evidence. A planned action is not described as performed. The current containment state has its own basis.
- `completed` eradication and `sent` notification each cite evidence. `pending` is not `sent`. Eradication, service recovery, and closure remain distinct.
- The chronology is append-only. Corrections identify the earlier event. Each event states its actor and authority basis, or states that the actor or authority basis is unknown. Secrets and raw sensitive indicator values are not copied into the document.
- Each cited project record exists and has a locator identifying the facts used in the incident record.
