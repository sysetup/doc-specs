# Data protection impact assessment specification

## Identity and selection

- **Specification ID:** `DPIA@core`.
- **Purpose:** Assess a bounded personal-data processing activity's necessity, proportionality, privacy risks to affected individuals, technical and organizational safeguards, consultation inputs, and residual-risk disposition across its lifecycle.
- **Intended readers:** Data controllers and product owners, privacy engineers and data protection officers, legal and compliance reviewers, security and data architects, and authorizing decision makers.
- **Decision or action supported:** Determine whether the processing activity is necessary and proportionate, whether safeguards reduce risks to affected individuals to an acceptable level, whether privacy-officer or supervisory-authority consultation is required, and whether processing may proceed, proceed with conditions, or remains blocked.
- **Use when:** A new or materially modified system, product feature, data pipeline, AI workflow, or organizational activity processes personal data—especially at scale, with sensitive attributes, profiling or automated decisioning, systematic monitoring, or cross-border transfers—requiring structured privacy-impact control.
- **Scope boundaries:** Control the privacy-impact analysis, safeguard status, consultation record, and residual-risk disposition for one bounded processing activity under the project's supplied privacy frameworks; exclude public privacy notices, legal-compliance certification, security-only threat modeling, and generic enterprise risk registers.

## Authoring inputs and unresolved facts

Obtain the processing activity identity, system or feature boundary, triggering event (new processing, material change, or scheduled review), as-of point, accountable controller or owner role, and current assessment state; the exact privacy statutes, regulations, contractual commitments, or organizational privacy standards actually supplied by the project (never inventing legal mandates); categories of affected individuals (including vulnerable groups when present), exact personal and special-category data fields, collection origins, end-to-end processing lifecycle (collection, storage, transformation, profiling or automated decisioning, sharing, cross-border transfer mechanisms, retention periods, and deletion or erasure workflows), and involved processors or recipients; stated processing purposes, supplied lawful or authorized processing basis, data-minimization and less-intrusive alternatives analysis, and operational mechanisms supporting data-subject rights; the project's declared privacy-risk assessment method and scales; identified privacy harm scenarios, existing verified controls, planned safeguards with owners and target dates, and projected versus observed residual risk; actual advice from a privacy officer, security reviewer, or affected-individual representatives, any applicable supervisory-authority prior-consultation status, actual disposition decisions with authority and conditions, and concrete triggers for reassessment.

If the supplied privacy framework, lawful or authorized basis, data retention schedule, subprocessor transfer boundary, data-subject rights workflow, risk scale, safeguard implementation status, or required consultation is unknown or undecided, record the gap explicitly with its impact on individuals, resolving action, and actual owner only when assigned. Do not invent statutory bases, legal advice, anonymization claims, completed safeguards, privacy-officer concurrence, or processing approval. Treat pseudonymized data as personal data unless verifiable irreversibility under the supplied framework is documented. Unresolved lawful basis, absent required safeguards for high privacy risk, or pending mandatory prior consultation blocks claiming an approved processing disposition.

## Finished-document contract

- **Title:** Name the system, feature, or personal-data processing activity and identify the document as its data protection impact assessment.
- **Frontmatter:** Required. Begin with a YAML 1.2 Semantic-Discovery frontmatter block delimited by `---` lines containing `title`, `description` (60 to 140 neutral retrieval words), `topics` (3 to 12 concrete lowercase kebab-case terms), `use_when` (2 to 6 neutral task conditions), and conditional `not_for` (1 to 5 grounded exclusions, omitted when empty), followed by the GFM H1 title. Exclude internal IDs, version strings, hashes, filesystem paths, build metadata, and unresolved template delimiters from frontmatter values. Processing scope, assessment state, residual risk, and disposition belong in the body. Governing source: Collection Semantic-Discovery Frontmatter contract.
- **Client or receiving party:** Conditional body element in the identity or scope section. Include it only when a named external party commissions or receives the work; then state that party's established name and relationship, such as client or receiving organization. Omit it for internal documents unless a specific project policy requires explicit negative attestation. Do not invent a party. Do not put the party in the specification ID, filename, or type name.
- **Order:** Establish assessment scope and supplied privacy framework before data inventory and flows; evaluate necessity, proportionality, and data-subject rights before privacy risks; place safeguards and residual risk before consultations, disposition, and review triggers. Heading wording may vary.
- **Presentation:** Use concise prose paired with structured data-flow tables, risk matrices, and safeguard trackers sized to the processing activity without adding empty sections for inapplicable transfers or automated decisioning. Required roles may share sections while keeping data categories, lawful-basis gaps, existing versus planned safeguards, residual risk, and actual disposition authority directly retrievable.

## Content definitions and forms

| Element | Meaning and cardinality | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Processing activity and assessment state | Exactly one bounded personal-data processing activity and one current state in Assessment scope, processing overview, and authority. | Use `draft`, `in-review`, `consultation-required`, `approved-with-conditions`, `approved`, `blocked`, or `superseded`. `approved` and `approved-with-conditions` require an actual recorded disposition from the assigned authority; completing the template alone stays `draft` or `in-review`. |
| Personal-data category and flow | One or more data categories mapped across collection, use, storage, sharing, retention, and deletion in Personal-data inventory and processing flows. | Identify sensitivity or special-category status, affected individual groups, and exact retention duration or unresolved retention gap; omit no known secondary use or logging copy. |
| Processor or transfer boundary | Zero or more external processors, joint controllers, recipients, or cross-border transfers in Personal-data inventory and processing flows. | State party role, data subset shared, jurisdiction or boundary, and supplied transfer mechanism; state assessed absence explicitly when processing is strictly local with no external sharing. |
| Necessity and rights disposition | One necessity and proportionality evaluation plus one operational-capability account per applicable data-subject right in Necessity, proportionality, and data-subject rights. | Cite supplied legal or governance authority for lawful basis; mark manual or missing rights-fulfillment paths explicitly rather than claiming compliance. |
| Privacy risk method and entry | One declared assessment method plus one or more privacy risk scenarios (or an assessed basis if none remain credible) in Privacy risk analysis. | Anchor severity to concrete harms to individuals (financial, physical, reputational, discrimination, loss of control), not solely organizational liability. Missing scales block numeric ratings. |
| Privacy safeguard | Zero or more technical or organizational mitigations per risk entry in Safeguards, mitigation status, and residual risk. | Distinguish `planned`, `implemented`, and `verified` states with owner and evidence locator; planned controls cannot be credited as observed risk reduction. |
| Residual privacy risk | One post-mitigation residual assessment per risk entry in Safeguards, mitigation status, and residual risk. | Label projected residual risk separately from observed post-verification reassessment. Residual risk remaining above the declared threshold triggers `consultation-required` or `blocked`. |
| Consultation record | Zero or more actual consultation inputs plus one explicit consultation-status statement in Consultations, disposition, and review triggers. | Name consulting role, date, advice or objections raised, and how each point was addressed or left open; never fabricate DPO or stakeholder concurrence. |
| Disposition and reassessment trigger | Zero or one actual authorization decision plus one or more concrete review triggers in Consultations, disposition, and review triggers. | Cite decision authority, scope, conditions, and date only when decided; enumerate material changes (new data field, new purpose, new subprocessor, AI retraining, incident) that reopen the DPIA. |

Use a personal-data inventory table (category, individuals, source, purpose, recipients, retention, deletion), a risk-to-safeguard register table (risk ID, scenario, pre-mitigation rating, safeguards and status, residual risk), and concise prose for necessity analysis, consultation outcomes, and disposition conditions. A data-flow diagram may clarify complex collection, transfer, and erasure paths; bind any external diagram by edition and locator. Do not include raw personal data samples, blank risk rows, or boilerplate legal opinions.

## Quality criteria

- Processing scope, system configuration, supplied privacy framework, assessment state, and accountable controller role bound every claim without fabricating legal certification.
- Personal-data inventory and flows account for all collected, derived, shared, transferred, retained, and deleted data elements, including secondary logs and third-party processors.
- Necessity, data minimization, lawful-basis provenance, less-intrusive alternatives, and operational data-subject rights mechanisms are assessed with missing workflows or undecided bases kept explicit.
- Privacy risks are evaluated under declared individual-harm criteria; missing scales leave ratings unresolved rather than invented.
- Implemented and verified safeguards remain distinguishable from planned actions, and projected residual risk remains distinguishable from observed post-implementation reassessment.
- Privacy-officer, stakeholder, and supervisory-authority consultation requirements, actual decisions, conditions, and concrete reassessment triggers are recorded transparently without equating completion of the assessment with processing approval.
