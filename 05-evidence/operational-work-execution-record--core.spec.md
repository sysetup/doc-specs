# Operational work execution record specification

## Identity and selection

- **Specification ID:** `OPERATIONAL-WORK-EXECUTION-RECORD@core`.
- **Purpose:** Record operational or maintenance work that was actually performed or attempted on a target, including the method and revision used, authorization, as-found state, actions, observed results, and return-to-service disposition when that disposition applies.
- **Intended readers:** Operators, maintainers, supervisors, asset or service owners, and investigators who need to know what was done to the target and the state in which it was left.
- **Decision or action supported:** Determine what work occurred, whether it stayed inside its authorization and method, what was observed before and after the actions, and whether the target was returned to service.
- **Use when:** Operational or maintenance work on a target has begun and the target, method, as-found state, actions, result, and any return-to-service disposition need durable evidence. Record an interrupted, failed, or unauthorized attempt as it occurred.
- **Scope boundaries:** One operational or maintenance work attempt on an actual target, with the actions performed, observations, and resulting operational state recorded.

## Authoring inputs and unresolved facts

Inspect the actual work window; the target and its boundary; every method actually used and its exact revision, or the fact that no controlled method was used; the work authorization, permit, or work order, or the fact that authorization is absent or unconfirmed; who performed or supervised the work; the observed condition before work; each action actually performed and its observed result; problems and departures from the method; the observed condition after those actions; return-to-service criteria and any actual disposition; and locators for retained evidence.

If a time, revision, observation, performer, or authorization is unknown, state the gap and its effect on the result. Do not invent commands, measurements, timestamps, people, or a procedure identifier. Do not paste the method text and present it as the action performed. Cite actual related project records and retained evidence by identity and locator. The work attempt must have started. Work still underway has no finish time and cannot be given a terminal result or a return-to-service grant. Omit secrets; if a retained command or log contains one, record the action and result without the secret.

## Finished-document contract

- **Title:** Identify the target and the work, and call the document an operational work execution record.
- **Frontmatter:** None. Begin with the GFM title. Target, method, authority, and result belong in the body.
- **Client or receiving party:** Conditional body element in the identity or scope section. Include it only when a named external party commissions or receives the work; then state that party's established name and relationship, such as client or receiving organization. When no external party exists, state that the document has no external client. Do not invent a party. Do not put the party in the specification ID, filename, or type name.
- **Order:** Identify the target, method, and authority before the actions. Place the overall result and any return-to-service disposition after the observations. Heading wording may vary.

| Section role | Status and inclusion condition | Content obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Work identity and authority | Required | Identify one bounded work event, the actual target, every method and exact revision used or the absence of a controlled method, and the authorization, its absence, or that it is unconfirmed. State the start time, or that it is unknown. Once the work has ended, state the finish or abort time, or that it is unknown. State who performed or supervised the work, or that the performer is unknown. |
| As-found state, actions, and observations | Required | State the observed condition before work, or that it was not observed. When the work depends on a configuration, revision, or environment, include the identity actually observed, or state that it was not observed. Record each action actually performed, in order when order matters, with its observed result and any retained evidence. State problems and both authorized and unauthorized departures. State the observed condition after the actions, or that it was not observed. |
| Result and return to service | Required | Give one overall result consistent with the actions, the authorization, and whether the work has ended. Do not treat the method's expected outcome as that result. When a controlled return-to-service disposition applies, state its criteria, evidence, status, and authorization. When it does not apply, say so. |

## Content definitions and forms

| Element | Meaning and cardinality | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Work event | One bounded operational or maintenance attempt. | Real work on the target must have started. Give the actual date or bounded period without inventing precision. |
| Target | One actual system, service, asset, component, location, or other controlled work target. | Identify it specifically enough to distinguish it from similar targets. A class is allowed only when the work applied to that whole class and the record says so. |
| Method | One or more methods actually used, or one explicit statement that no controlled method was used. | Identify each runbook, standard operating procedure, work instruction, maintenance task, work-order method, or other basis by title or identifier, exact revision, and locator when one exists. When no controlled method was used, say so and do not invent a procedure identifier. When more than one revision was used, tie each action to the revision it followed. The method remains instruction; its text is not the action performed. |
| Work authorization | One authorization, permit, approval, or work order when one governed the work; otherwise one explicit absence or unconfirmed state. | State its scope and limits. Missing or unconfirmed authorization stays visible and cannot be written as an authorized claim or as `completed`. |
| Time | One actual start, or one explicit unknown. One finish or abort time once the event has ended, or one explicit unknown. | Use a date-time with a timezone, and preserve the observed precision. Work still underway has no finish time. Do not invent a timestamp. |
| Performer | One or more roles or identities that performed or supervised the work, or one explicit unknown. | Limit identifying detail to the permitted audience and supplied disclosure restrictions. When more than one actor participated and the difference matters, state who did what. Do not invent a person or role. |
| As-found state | One observed pre-work condition, or one explicit not-observed statement. | Include retained evidence locators when they exist. Include an observed configuration, revision, or environment identity when the work depends on it. The expected or nominal state does not substitute for the observation. |
| Action | Zero or more actions actually performed. | Each has a stable identity within the record, the action performed, the observed result, and evidence locators when evidence was retained. Measurements include units and uncertainty when the observation has them. Zero actions is allowed only when no method action was performed and the overall result is `in-progress`, `aborted`, or `inconclusive`. `completed`, `completed-with-exceptions`, and `failed` each require at least one action. Unperformed method steps are not actions. |
| End state | One observed condition after the recorded actions, or one explicit not-observed statement. | Keep it distinct from the as-found state, the intended state, and the method's expected postcondition. A return-to-service claim cannot treat an unobserved intended state as the observed end state. |
| Problem | Zero or more problems or defects observed during the work. | Describe each one. Cite a separate problem or incident record only when that record exists. |
| Departure | Zero or more departures from the method. | Keep an authorized deviation or waiver, with its authority, distinct from an unauthorized departure. A missing waiver does not make a departure authorized. |
| Overall result | One of `in-progress`, `completed`, `completed-with-exceptions`, `aborted`, `failed`, or `inconclusive`. | `in-progress` means the work has not ended. `completed` means the intended operational result was achieved, authorization was confirmed and within scope, and there is no exception. `completed-with-exceptions` means that result was achieved and each exception is named, including missing or unconfirmed authorization or an unauthorized departure. Do not use either completed value when the intended result was not achieved. `aborted` means the work ended before the intended end of the method; state the stop point and why. A failed step may explain the abort, and an intended step that was not run is not an action. `failed` means the attempt reached its intended end or its defined failure point and the intended result was not achieved. When an early stop and a missed result both apply, use `aborted` if intended steps were not run and `failed` if the planned attempt finished without the result. `inconclusive` means the observations do not support a result. Use one primary value and keep any accompanying condition visible. |
| Return to service | One disposition when a controlled return applies; otherwise one explicit `not-applicable` statement. | When it applies, status is `pending`, `returned-to-service`, or `not-returned`. `returned-to-service` requires a terminal result other than `inconclusive`, the criteria or basis actually used, the checks or evidence, an observed end state or checks that support the disposition, and the actual authorizing decision when the project requires one. `pending` and `not-returned` remain visible. When the status is `not-applicable`, omit criteria and a return authorization rather than filling them. |

Use a short identity block for the target, method, authority, and times. Use a table or ordered entries for actions, with the action, observed result, and evidence locator as columns when several actions differ. Use prose for the as-found state, the end state, authority limits, and the return-to-service basis. A timeline is appropriate when sequence or overlap changes the meaning. Do not add a blank action row, a document-lifecycle block, or a generic completion checklist.

## Quality criteria

- A reader can identify the target; the method revision actually used, or that no controlled method was used; who acted, or that the performer is unknown; and what was observed before and after the actions, or that an observation is missing.
- The method, the authorization, and the observed actions remain separate facts. Missing authorization, a missing as-found or end-state observation, and an unauthorized departure stay visible.
- The overall result matches the actions, the authorization, and whether the work has ended. An achieved result with missing authorization is `completed-with-exceptions`, not `completed`. An early stop that leaves intended steps unrun is `aborted`; a finished attempt that misses the result is `failed`. Exceptions, aborts, failures, and open work remain visible.
- Return-to-service status is supported by the stated criteria, observed end state or checks, evidence, and required authorization.
